A leaderboard is readable only if four things hold
--------------------------------------------------

  1. actor-disjointness   the gold producers are not the contestants
  2. common denominator   the floor and the board are computed on the same items
  3. multi-generator      at least three gold producers
  4. power-bounded        the margin exceeds what this sample can resolve


Tenet 3 and 4: five generators, a clear separation
--------------------------------------------------

  five generators, agreement 0.93
    verdict  : READABLE
    floor    : 0.9210 (loo, m=5, n=400)
    margin   : +0.0390   threshold: 0.0274 = max(delta=0.02, mde=0.0274)

    READABLE. But note what that word does not mean: it says the
    gap is larger than the measurement error. It says nothing about
    whether the measurement is the right one.

Tenet 3: two generators are not enough
--------------------------------------

  two generators
    verdict  : UNDECIDABLE
    reason   : floor_degenerate

    With m=2, leave-one-out leaves a single rater: 'agreement with
    the others' degenerates into agreement with itself. There is no
    estimator, so the honest verdict is UNDECIDABLE, not a number.

Tenet 1: the board and the floor must share their items
-------------------------------------------------------

  floor on 400 items, board on 150
    verdict  : UNDECIDABLE
    reason   : domain_mismatch

    Two different denominators. The difference between the two
    statistics is not an effect size, it is an artefact of which items
    each was computed on.

Tenet 2: the judge cannot be one of the contestants
---------------------------------------------------

  'strong' is also a gold generator
    verdict  : UNDECIDABLE
    reason   : actors_overlap

    If a contestant helped define the gold, its own score partly
    measures how well it agrees with itself.

Tenet 4: the margin has to beat what the sample can resolve
-----------------------------------------------------------

  same board, only 120 items
    verdict  : MARGINAL
    floor    : 0.9333 (loo, m=5, n=120)
    margin   : +0.0267   threshold: 0.0501 = max(delta=0.02, mde=0.0501)
    reason   : margin +0.0267 is inside the resolution bound 0.0501

    The gap did not change; the resolution did. A number below the
    minimum detectable effect is not a small effect, it is no
    measurement at all.

The trap: gold producers that carry no signal
---------------------------------------------

  five generators agreeing only by chance
    verdict  : UNDECIDABLE
    reason   : floor_degenerate

    floor vs chance : {'z': -1.4399999999999968, 'critical_z': 1.9599639845400536, 'se': 0.025, 'n_items': 400, 'n_items_needed': None, 'note': 'the floor (0.4640) is at or below the chance level (0.5000); no sample size makes it informative', 'above_chance': False}

    This is the one that matters. Five independent coin-flippers agree with
    each other about half the time, so the floor sits near 0.5 -- and any
    score above that looks like a landslide. A margin-only check calls it
    READABLE with a huge margin. The floor is tested against the chance rate
    it would have if the generators carried no signal at all, and when it
    cannot be distinguished from chance the verdict is UNDECIDABLE.

    The resolution comes from the number of ITEMS, not the number of
    generators: the floor's variability is driven by which items were drawn,
    and that variation is shared by every generator.
    n_items_needed to call this floor above chance: None
    the floor (0.4640) is at or below the chance level (0.5000); no sample size makes it informative

Unpaired vs paired resolution: read mde_method
----------------------------------------------
  without per-item scores : mde=0.0274 (binomial_approx)  -> READABLE
  with per-item scores    : mde=0.0743 (cluster_bootstrap)  -> MARGINAL

    Same board, same generators, same items. The verdict moved from
    READABLE to MARGINAL because the MDE moved from
    0.0274 to 0.0743, and nothing about the data changed.

    The fallback treats the leader's accuracy as an independent binomial
    proportion. The paired bootstrap resamples items and keeps the two systems'
    outcomes on the same item together, so it sees the correlation the fallback
    assumes away. When the two systems agree item-by-item more than chance
    would predict, the paired difference has a *larger* standard error than the
    independent approximation suggests -- the fallback was optimistic.

    An MDE that quietly answers a different question than the one asked is the
    failure this package exists to make visible. It is not resolved by picking
    the friendlier number; it is resolved by naming the method, which is why
    ``mde_method`` is always in the report.
